ABOUT THIS PLACE

Here, outside social media, away from competition, worry of perception, or misrepresenting the moment, is where we come to explore creative acts and the act of making. I believe that creative acts come from all corners of life, and each can reflect beauty and intention.

The mediums of content represented here come in a wide variety of forms and figures. Whether that be written, visual, physical, philosophical, or even just a passing moment caught between strangers that felt striking, sources of inspiration for me can come from anywhere. My studies and work in aerospace are seemingly quantitative and technical, but they can often leave me mesmerized and inspired.

Additionally, the outdoors serve as a never-ending source of newness and life. I am constantly trying to find that tantalizing feeling of being small within something great, big, and beautiful—and experiencing the feelings these wild places can evoke.

This site was built as an archive and display of the content and experiences I find in my daily life, create myself, or explore with others. All the writing, visuals, and reflections are my own unless otherwise specified. Although this means they are imperfect at times, that also makes them real and personal.

In an effort to avoid machine-made art and rigidity, this space is a breeding ground for pure, exploratory creating for the joy of creating.

My Story

Loving the outdoors has been ingrained in me since day one. I grew up in the mountains of Bend, Oregon, and had the privilege of skiing before class, climbing at sunrise, taking week-long river trips in the summers, hiking outside my door, and living within a community that valued the wild.

The Deschutes River ran directly through town. Through frequent family trips on this river, the Rogue River, the Salmon River—both the Main and Middle Fork—and the McKenzie River, I began to see incredible symbolism in the movement and life of rivers.

When leaving Bend for college, I found myself stumped on what could be better than the mountain town where I grew up. I knew that several grueling academic years were ahead of me as a soon-to-be aerospace engineering major, so I accepted that skiing would not be part of my daily life as it had been.

Given this, in combination with looking to study a niche subject, I landed in San Luis Obispo, California, at Cal Poly. Along the way, there have been a plethora of adventures and new places.

Given how I spend my free time and the places where I grew up, aerospace came somewhat from left field. I was introduced to the subject in a high-school physics class when we were taught how a jet engine worked. I was hooked.

I had always enjoyed mathematics and the sciences, and flight felt like magic—whether experienced from the ground or in the air. I was excited to learn how this seemingly mystical science could be explained through numbers and theory.

Now, years later, as I wrap up my bachelor's degree with a master's on the horizon, I am still uncovering that magic. Work experience has given me the opportunity to become more hands-on with aircraft and closer to flight than I could have imagined. I cannot wait to keep chasing the pixie dust.

Amidst all of this, I frequently find myself within the arts. Both of my parents are creatives—photographers, graphic designers, ceramicists, cooks, and practitioners of many more niche arts that I have had the pleasure of witnessing over the years.

I was often crafty growing up, whether that meant crochet, painting, collage, pottery, mixed media, or something else. Now, while balancing various parts of my life alongside creative outlets, I find myself doing many of the same things and exploring a few new artistic hobbies.

Crochet and collage are still high on the list, as well as writing poetry, drawing, watercolor, block printing, digital art, photography, or whatever feels intriguing.
